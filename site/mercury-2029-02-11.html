Where it can be seen

sees the occultation graze limit city sees it near miss

The gold line is the north limit: along it Mercury grazes the Moon's northern edge. North of the line it is a near miss — from Jaipur Mercury passes just 0.1′ outside the limb — and a graze seen from right on the line, Mercury blinking in and out behind lunar mountains, is the finest view of all. The solid line is traced against the real lunar limb — LRO LOLA terrain, the mountains and valleys that are on the edge for this libration — and sits -10.9 to +2.2 km from the mean-sphere line (dotted).

How to watch

Method & accuracy

Positions from the JPL DE431 ephemeris (Skyfield), ΔT = 69.1 s. The city table's contact times and the solid limit line use the real lunar limb — the silhouette of LRO LOLA terrain (LDEM_64, ~0.5 km) at each observer's own libration — so they carry the mountains and valleys that a mean sphere misses (up to a few seconds at ordinary contacts, tens of seconds near a graze). The any-location solver in your browser and the shaded region still use the mean sphere of 1737.4 km (±2 s). Mercury's own disc (7″ across) is included: partial contacts are the from/to times.
