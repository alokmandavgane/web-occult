Reading the list

Times are for the moment the moon's centre crosses the edge of Jupiter or of its shadow, the convention of published tables; the moon's own disc takes a few minutes to cross. A start or end marked unseen happens behind Jupiter or inside its shadow. “Only what I can see” keeps events with Jupiter more than 10° up in a dark sky. Positions are from JPL DE431 and the jup365 satellite ephemeris, Jupiter as an oblate spheroid with its shadow cone. Mutual-event brightness drops assume uniform discs and are estimates. Events are geocentric — the same instant everywhere on Earth to well under a second. The Great Red Spot is a storm, not ephemeris: its times are when it crosses the middle of the disc (the central meridian, System II from DE431), taking its longitude as 91° on 1 Jun 2026 drifting 1.75° a month (Sky & Telescope, from the JUPOS drift record). Each degree that estimate is off moves the time by 1.65 min; the spot is well placed for about 50 minutes either side.
